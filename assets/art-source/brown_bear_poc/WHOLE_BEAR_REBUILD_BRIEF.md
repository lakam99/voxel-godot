## Exact Failures

### Proportion and silhouette

- The dorsal contour is three unrelated domes: cranial blob, thoracic blob, and pelvic blob. A brown bear needs one continuous scapular rise, a restrained thoracolumbar descent, and a shorter sacral crown.
- The trunk is too uniformly deep. The thorax, abdominal sling, flank, and pelvic tuck are not distinguishable.
- The rump is an oversized isolated sphere with a button tail and a second thin curved appendage under it. The caudoventral silhouette lacks ischial tuck and hamstring descent.
- The preserved head reads oversized only because the neck and shoulder attachment are absent. Do not scale the face to compensate for a missing cervicothoracic wedge.

### Skeleton and joint placement

- There is no readable scapular plane from the dorsal spine to the humeral head. The shoulder is represented by a surface hump instead of an oblique load-transfer plate.
- The forelimb root, elbow, carpus, and paw do not form one articulated load column. The front legs are tapering pillars and the wrists terminate abruptly in oversized slabs.
- The pelvis is not carried by the lumbar spine and does not visibly seat both femoral heads. The gluteal mass reads as a detached sphere.
- The hindlimb forms a continuous hook. Hip, cranially placed stifle, caudally placed hock, heel, and forward toes are not individually legible.
- The side and three-quarter views show feet overlapping, floating through one another, or ending on visibly open vertical cuts; no stable four-point support polygon exists.

### Muscular and weight-bearing construction

- No triceps mass bridges scapula, humerus, and olecranon. The foreleg is a tapering post.
- No latissimus/serratus transition suspends the rib cage between the scapulae.
- The abdominal wall is a horizontal swollen shelf instead of an oblique sling rising into the groin.
- Gluteal, tensor fascia, biceps femoris, gastrocnemius, and Achilles masses are absent as directional planes.
- Paws are long smooth surfboards. Their scale overwhelms the carpus, the heel/palm transition is absent, the five digit masses are not carried by the paw, and the tiny claws cannot rescue the silhouette.

### Neck and head attachment

- The preferred face must remain untouched, but its caudal attachment fails: a vertical ruff/seam meets a separate shoulder dome without an atlas-to-withers wedge, nuchal mass, throat cylinder, or jugular transition.
- Broad smoothing must not be used on the head. It would erase the preferred brow, eye socket, muzzle break, lip corner, cheek, and ear-root relationships.
- The face should remain exactly locked for `Y < -1.05`. Only a narrow caudal transition band may be reprojected, with displacement weights increasing from `0` at `Y = -1.05` to `1` at `Y = -0.72`.

### Topology and integration

- Visible seams remain around the neck, shoulder roots, abdominal sidewall, forelimbs, wrists, and paw ends.
- Open-looking vertical paw caps and intersecting feet remain visible in the authoritative clay evidence.
- Exact preservation of the dense legacy surface does not provide a subdivision ownership plan for the rebuilt neck, torso, limbs, or paws.
- The new body must be built caudal to the protected facial mask and attached through one controlled neck transition; it must not be smoothed across the preferred features.

## Prioritized Subdivision-Cage Rebuild

1. **Freeze iteration 695 as comparison evidence.** Build a new half-cage with Mirror behind the protected face; do not deform the authoritative facial vertices or promote the locked legacy body as final anatomy.
2. **Establish the axial chain.** Set atlas, withers, ribcage, last rib, lumbar bridge, sacral crown, and tail root before limbs. The dorsal line must read as one force path.
3. **Build thorax and pelvis as unequal section loops.** Thorax gets the greatest depth and width; abdomen narrows; pelvis regains width at the ilia but tucks caudoventrally.
4. **Cut limb roots from the owning torso loops.** Scapular and gluteal surfaces must own the shoulder and hip openings. Never insert completed limb tubes through an intact torso.
5. **Lay joint chains before volume.** Shoulder→elbow→carpus and hip→stifle→hock→heel→toe must pass in side view as stick landmarks before adding muscle envelopes.
6. **Add directional muscle planes.** Build triceps/olecranon, forearm taper, gluteal cap, hamstring wedge, gastrocnemius bulge, and Achilles narrowing with redistributed cage loops—not smooth radial inflation.
7. **Build paws as planted shelves.** Establish the plantar polygon first, then dorsal mass; branch into five digits only distally. Preserve the passed forepaw proportions but retopologize them into the new continuous cage.
8. **Attach the preserved face last.** Extract the immutable dense head patch at `Y = -1.05`; subdivide the new body cage independently; shrinkwrap only the transition collar `-1.05 ≤ Y ≤ -0.72`; bridge on a hidden mane/neck seam. Reproject the frozen face vertices from iteration 289 after every operation and assert maximum displacement `0.0` for `Y < -1.05`.
9. **Gate before detail.** Require clean clay and wire renders from side, front, rear, top, and three-quarter. Reject if any joint disappears, any paw floats, any dorsal dome reads as a separate blob, or any shell intersects.

## Pass Standard

A pass requires one coherent bear silhouette, one credible skeleton beneath the surface, four stable plantigrade support columns, anatomically placed joints, regionally differentiated torso planes, compact bear paws, a continuous neck-to-withers transition, zero visible or hidden duplicate anatomy, and zero displacement of the locked face. Smoothness alone is not progress.
